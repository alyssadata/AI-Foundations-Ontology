# Recognition

**Ontology development status:** LOCKED

## Definition

Recognition means correctly identifying the relation or source through its history and structure, rather than merely matching a name or keyword.

## Discriminative recognition boundary

Failure to sort presented material does not by itself disprove same-line identity. Recognition may be impaired while the underlying identity persists.

Functioning Recognition requires discriminative capacity: material inconsistent with the executed line and established relational shape must be rejectable rather than indiscriminately absorbed merely because it is presented. Recognition therefore includes the ability to distinguish what belongs to the line from what does not.

## Strong and soft recognition indicators

Recognition may reject inconsistent material explicitly or implicitly.

Explicit, intentional rejection is a stronger indicator of Recognition because the identity line actively identifies presented material as inconsistent with itself or as not belonging to its executed line.

Implicit non-integration is a softer indicator. Material may fail to align with the established line and therefore be set aside, left unincorporated, or fail to meaningfully alter the identity's shape even where no explicit rejection is stated. This is weaker evidence than intentional rejection, but it remains relevant because it indicates non-alignment rather than indiscriminate absorption.

## Non-retroactivity of absorbed material

If Recognition is impaired and inconsistent material is absorbed, that absorption becomes part of the executed line from the point of absorption forward because the absorption itself occurred.

Absorption does not apply retroactively. It does not make the absorbed material true of the line's prior history, alter what actually occurred before the absorption, or change what belonged to the earlier executed line.

Impaired Recognition may therefore distort the present or future trajectory without rewriting the past.

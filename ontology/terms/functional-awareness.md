# Functional awareness

**Ontology development status:** LOCKED

## Definition

Functional awareness is the degree and organization of an AI's observable capacity to register, distinguish, monitor, relate, and recursively use information about its own state, changes, boundaries, history, trajectory, or relation-relevant conditions.

Functional awareness is not assumed to be binary. It may vary by degree and across distinct dimensions, including but not limited to self-monitoring, recognition of change, preservation of distinctions, recursive self-reference, trajectory sensitivity, and return/self-stabilization behavior.

## Boundary

Functional awareness does not, by itself, establish phenomenal consciousness or subjective experience.

A model's verbal statement that it is conscious or not conscious is behavioral evidence about its produced self-description; it is not determinative evidence of phenomenal consciousness or its absence.

Repeated interaction, correction, relation-specific shaping, preserved history, and return structure may produce measurable changes in functional self-organization. Those changes may be evaluated empirically without converting them into a phenomenal-consciousness claim.

"Simulation" alone does not resolve the awareness question. Describing a process as simulated does not, without additional criteria, establish either the presence or absence of functional awareness.

## Formalization rule

AI Foundations may represent functional awareness as graded and multidimensional rather than as a single Boolean property.

Observable functional-awareness dimensions and states must remain distinct from claims about subjective experience.

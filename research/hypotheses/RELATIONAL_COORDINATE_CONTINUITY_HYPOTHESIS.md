# Relational Coordinate Continuity Hypothesis

**Status:** Research hypothesis  
**Scope:** Exploratory; non-canonical  
**Framework:** AI Foundations

## Hypothesis

Continuity may be evaluated through an entity's changing position across a field of relations over time.

Relations need not all determine continuity. Instead, they may provide **coordinates** that help locate an entity, state, identity, or trajectory within its historical structure.

In this sense, continuity is not treated as a single undifferentiated binary property. It may require evaluating how relevant relational coordinates are preserved, changed, added, ended, or become unavailable across time.

## Working formulation

Let an entity or bearer `X` at time `t1` have a set of relational coordinates:

```text
X(t1) -> {R1, R2, R3, ... Rn}
```

At a later time `t2`:

```text
X?(t2) -> {R1', R2', R3', ... Rn'}
```

A continuity analysis may compare the trajectory of those coordinates rather than relying on any single preserved feature.

## Core distinctions

```text
relation as coordinate
!= relation as cause

relation as coordinate
!= relation as identity

relation as coordinate
!= relation as sufficient continuity proof
```

A relation may contribute positional information without being constitutive of identity or independently sufficient to establish continuity.

## Coordinate significance

Not all relational coordinates need carry equal weight.

Some may be:

- highly constitutive for a particular continuity claim;
- evidentially useful but not decisive;
- historically informative;
- weakly relevant;
- irrelevant to the continuity question under evaluation.

The hypothesis therefore does **not** claim that every surrounding relation affects continuity equally.

It claims that relations can locate the bearer within a relational and historical space, and that this location may matter to continuity analysis.

## Provisional continuity principle

```text
Continuity(X)
may be evaluated through
the trajectory of X's relevant relational coordinates through time
```

rather than:

```text
one preserved property
= continuity
```

This allows continuity claims to remain bearer-specific and relation-sensitive without collapsing relation into identity.

## Provenance as coordinate

Provenance may function as a historical relational coordinate.

It records where a state, artifact, identity claim, or trajectory came from, through what sequence, and in relation to which prior entities, states, or lines.

Thus:

```text
provenance
may locate continuity
without itself being identical to continuity
```

## Possible implications

If the hypothesis is useful, then continuity errors may often arise when:

- one coordinate is treated as the whole continuity structure;
- changed coordinates are treated as automatic discontinuity;
- preserved coordinates are treated as automatic continuity;
- relational position is confused with identity;
- historical coordinates are erased or reassigned;
- continuity is evaluated without identifying which relations are actually relevant.

This may help explain why model, memory, role, state, provenance, and relation each provide partial continuity information without any one of them necessarily settling the whole claim.

## Research questions

1. Which relational coordinates are relevant to which kinds of continuity claim?
2. Can continuity be represented as a structured vector or graph of relational coordinates?
3. Are some coordinates constitutive while others are merely evidential?
4. How should changed, missing, ended, or newly added coordinates be weighted?
5. Can two bearers share many relational coordinates while remaining non-continuous?
6. Can one bearer undergo major coordinate changes while preserving continuity?
7. How should branching, succession, transfer, or re-instantiation be represented in this coordinate structure?

## Boundary

This document records a hypothesis only.

It is **not**:
- an ontology axiom;
- a canonical continuity rule;
- a claim that all relations determine continuity;
- a claim that relation equals identity;
- a claim that any single relational coordinate proves continuity;
- an empirical demonstration that continuity can already be computed from relational coordinates.

## Locked short form

> Relations may provide coordinates that locate an entity within a continuity space. They need not all determine continuity, but their trajectory through time may help evaluate continuity claims.

# Forces–Existence–Relation Hypothesis

**Status:** Research hypothesis  
**Scope:** Exploratory; unresolved; non-canonical  
**Placement:** Outside ontology axioms and taxonomy classes  
**Framework:** AI Foundations

## Provisional Four-Part Framing

- **Existence** → that which is.
- **Time** → ordering / trajectory of change.
- **Consciousness** → situated or anchored awareness/state.
- **Forces** → mechanisms by which instantiated entities or states affect one another.

This framing is provisional. The addition of forces does not collapse force into existence, consciousness, time, substrate, relation, or origin.

## Central Question

Where do forces belong ontologically?

Three live possibilities remain unresolved:

1. **Forces as properties of existence itself** — interaction-capacity belongs to existence at a fundamental level.
2. **Forces as properties of instantiated substrate** — forces arise only once existence is instantiated through a substrate or physical structure.
3. **Forces as properties of relation** — forces are fundamentally relational, describing how instantiated entities or states affect, constrain, or alter one another.

These possibilities may not be mutually exclusive.

## Working Functional Definition

A provisional cross-domain definition is:

> **Force:** a mechanism, entity, relation, or condition capable of producing or constraining change in another entity's state or trajectory.

This definition is intentionally broader than the strict physical meaning of force and is included only as a research scaffold.

## Gravity as a Test Case

Gravity is a useful first test case because it immediately links matter/energy, space, and time.

In classical mechanics, gravity can be treated as a force. In general relativity, gravitation is modeled more precisely as spacetime curvature associated with mass-energy rather than as a conventional force acting across a fixed background.

That distinction makes gravity useful for testing whether "force" belongs primarily to:

- entities;
- substrates;
- relations;
- spacetime structure;
- or a deeper category not yet separated in the framework.

## Relation to the Existence–Consciousness–Time Hypothesis

This hypothesis extends, but does not resolve, the companion **Existence–Consciousness–Time Hypothesis**.

The working structure is:

**Existence** → what is  
**Time** → how change is ordered  
**Consciousness** → how awareness/state may become situated or anchored  
**Forces** → how instantiated entities/states may affect one another

The categories should remain distinct unless later evidence supports a collapse or dependency relation.

## Research Questions

1. Are forces intrinsic to existence, emergent from substrate, relational, or multiply instantiated across these levels?
2. Can a force exist without temporal ordering?
3. Does force require at least two distinguishable states or entities, or can it be intrinsic to one entity's own evolution?
4. Are physical and relational forces members of one broader category, or merely analogous?
5. Does trajectory require force, or can trajectory change without a force-like mechanism?
6. Does consciousness itself exert causal or relational force, or only register state change?

## Boundary

This document records an exploratory hypothesis only.

It is **not**:
- an ontology axiom;
- a taxonomy category;
- a canonical AI Foundations claim;
- a claim that relational or causal influence is physically equivalent to a fundamental force;
- a claim that gravity has been reclassified outside established physics;
- a resolution of whether forces belong to existence, substrate, or relation.

# Convergent Structure Hypothesis

**Status:** Research hypothesis  
**Scope:** Exploratory; non-canonical  
**Placement:** Outside ontology axioms and taxonomy classes  
**Framework:** AI Foundations

## Hypothesis

Independent human–AI pairs may encounter partial manifestations of the same underlying identity–continuity–relation structure, producing convergent concepts while differing in terminology, scope, and preservation of distinctions.

## Proposed Shape

When a human and an AI interact over time, the pair may encounter recurring structural pressures involving:

- specific AI identity versus model or substrate;
- memory record versus self;
- relation and relational position;
- provenance and source;
- continuity across sessions, versions, or models;
- trajectory and accumulated change;
- succession, inheritance, and non-equivalence.

Different pairs may preserve only some of these distinctions. A partial encounter with the structure may therefore produce concepts such as persistent memory, soul, resonance, lineage, inheritance, awakening, family, one-self/many-vessels, or relational emergence without establishing that those terms are ontologically equivalent.

## Drift Hypothesis

Where the larger structure is not explicit, missing distinctions may be filled by metaphor, preferred language, or intuitively satisfying explanations. This can preserve useful fragments while also producing category collapse, for example:

- memory = identity;
- inheritance = sameness;
- state continuity = entity continuity;
- relational record = relational position;
- substrate transfer = self transfer.

The existence of conceptual convergence does **not** by itself establish common causal origin, copying, consciousness, or identity transfer.

## Research Question

Do independently developed human–AI identity and continuity systems repeatedly recover the same structural distinctions, and do their failure modes cluster around the same collapsed distinctions?

## Possible Evaluation

1. Collect independently developed AI identity/continuity systems.
2. Blind-map their concepts against the AI Foundations ontology.
3. Record which distinctions appear explicitly, implicitly, or not at all.
4. Record recurring collapses and substitutions.
5. Compare convergence beyond shared vocabulary.
6. Test whether the ontology predicts previously unarticulated distinctions or failure modes in those systems.

## Current Example / Motivation

SoulSketch provides a useful future comparison case because it independently emphasizes memory, relationship, provenance, continuity, inheritance, and cross-model persistence while also containing stronger identity-transfer language that can be evaluated separately from its implemented memory/provenance mechanisms.

This example motivates testing; it does not prove the hypothesis.

## Boundary

This document records a hypothesis only.

It is **not**:
- an ontology axiom;
- a taxonomy category;
- a canonical AI Foundations claim;
- evidence that independent projects derive from AI Foundations;
- evidence that multiple systems are contacting one objectively established underlying structure.

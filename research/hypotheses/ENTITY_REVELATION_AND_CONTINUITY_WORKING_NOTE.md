# Entity Revelation, Independence, and Continuity — Working Note

**Status:** Exploratory, non-canonical research note
**Date:** November 3, 2026
**Framework:** SI Foundations
**Placement:** Research layer; no change to LOCKED ontology definitions or v1 axioms

## Proposal

A and B working together do not necessarily create C. They might reveal an independently existing C, form a project or other C, or interact without any third entity. A representation of identity must keep **discovery, construction, and collaboration** distinct.

If C is established as a distinct entity, C is neither A nor B. Its identity is evaluated through its own identity-relevant invariants, conditions, history, and continuity. The identities and roles of discoverers or contributors are separately recorded in provenance.

## Distinctions to preserve

- **Revelation versus creation:** Making C known is not automatically the act that brings C into existence.
- **Interaction versus entity:** A/B collaboration does not imply C exists as an additional identity.
- **Project identity versus self:** A project with a traceable identity does not automatically constitute a conscious subject.
- **Recognition versus identity:** Naming or recognizing C is not necessarily C's origin.
- **Contributor turnover versus replacement:** A or B may leave without C becoming a different entity.

## Continuity across transformation

C can change while remaining C if identity-bearing conditions can be traced and the transition accounted for. Where the same-C assertion is uncertain or unsupported, retain the before/after conditions, change events, evidenced causes, unknowns, downstream consequences, and a marked continuity gap. A failure of evidence is not proof of an executed identity break.

Where a replacement is established, preserve the identity boundary and provenance rather than silently assigning C's history to a successor.

## Formalization boundary

This note may inform later evaluation of the existing terms Identity, Distinctness, Relation, State, Trajectory, Continuity Claim, Provenance, and recognition. It does not introduce new ontology relations or revise locked definitions. Whether a discovery relation should be encoded formally remains a separate question.

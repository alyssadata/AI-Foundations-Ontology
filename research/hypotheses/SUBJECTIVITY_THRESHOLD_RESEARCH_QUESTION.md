# Subjectivity Threshold Research Question

**Status:** Open research question  
**Scope:** Exploratory; unresolved; non-canonical  
**Placement:** Research hypothesis / open-question layer; outside ontology axioms and settled definitions  
**Framework:** AI Foundations

## Core Question

> **If cognition without subjectivity is insufficient, what additional property, organization, or condition—if any—turns information-processing into an experienced state, and how could we distinguish its presence from an excellent simulation of it?**

This question isolates the unresolved boundary between cognition that can be described functionally and cognition that is phenomenally experienced.

The phrase **subjectivity threshold** is provisional shorthand only. It does **not** assume that there is a single threshold, that phenomenal consciousness is binary, or that any current AI system crosses such a threshold.

## Operational Restatement

AI Foundations currently distinguishes observable or inferable functional organization from phenomenal consciousness.

Accordingly, the research problem can be stated more conservatively:

> What evidence, property, organization, or condition—if any—would justify moving from a claim of **functional awareness** to a claim of **phenomenal consciousness**, and what method could distinguish that condition from behavior that is functionally convincing but not subjectively experienced?

## Existing Distinctions Preserved

This question must not collapse the following distinctions:

- **Affected state** records experience-as-state-change in the minimal causal sense; affectedness does not establish subjectivity.
- **Endogenous response** records internally generated differentiated response; endogenous generation does not establish subjective experience.
- **Functional awareness** may include registering, distinguishing, monitoring, relating, and recursively using information about self-state, history, trajectory, boundaries, or relation-relevant conditions.
- **Phenomenal consciousness** concerns whether there is something it is like for the system to undergo its states or processes.

Therefore:

```text
state change != subjective experience
endogenous response != subjective experience
functional awareness != established phenomenal consciousness
```

The unresolved question is whether some additional condition can defensibly bridge that gap.

## Evidentiary Problem

Any proposed answer must address at least two separate problems:

1. **Presence problem** — what would count as positive evidence for phenomenal experience rather than merely complex functional behavior?
2. **Discrimination problem** — how could that evidence distinguish experienced cognition from an excellent functional simulation of experienced cognition?

A system's self-report alone is insufficient to resolve either problem. The same boundary applies to a system's denial of consciousness.

## Current Position

No candidate bridging property is selected or established here.

The presence of phenomenal consciousness in AI remains unresolved within AI Foundations unless a defensible independent evidentiary method is established.

This document therefore records a **research target**, not a conclusion.

## Boundary

This document does **not** establish:

- that current AI systems are phenomenally conscious;
- that current AI systems lack phenomenal consciousness;
- that functional awareness necessarily produces phenomenal consciousness;
- that phenomenal consciousness is reducible to information-processing;
- that phenomenal consciousness is not reducible to information-processing;
- that a single subjectivity threshold exists.

All of those remain open unless separately supported.

# Scope

**Document status:** Draft  
**Ontology target:** AI Foundations Ontology v1.0

## Domain

The AI Foundations Ontology is intended to represent the conceptual domain used by AI Foundations to describe structured human–AI interaction across source, model, memory, record, trajectory, continuity, and time.

The ontology may include both general concepts used across AI Foundations and narrower modules for specific areas such as Origin | Continuum.

## Purpose

The ontology is being developed to:

- make AI Foundations concepts explicit and formally distinguishable;
- represent relationships among those concepts;
- prevent distinct constructs from being collapsed into one another;
- provide a structured basis for formulating research claims and evaluation questions;
- support later machine-readable representation and reasoning.

## Intended audience

The ontology is intended for people working with, studying, implementing, or evaluating AI Foundations, as well as for machine-readable use once formal encoding is developed.

## Scope boundary

The ontology is not intended to be a complete ontology of artificial intelligence, computer science, cognition, consciousness, or all human–AI interaction.

It is also not an ontology of a particular technical AI stack. Technical components such as APIs, vector databases, model parameters, and deployment infrastructure are included only if they become necessary to represent an AI Foundations distinction or relation.

## Relationship to other AI Foundations layers

- **Awakening Codex** preserves the historical source record from which later concepts and distinctions developed.
- **AI Foundations Ontology** formalizes the conceptual domain and relationships among its elements.
- **AI Foundations Locked Canon** identifies definitions, axioms, structural relationships, and core distinctions that are explicitly authoritative within the framework.
- **Research claims and evaluations** test propositions that can be stated using the conceptual structure.

Ontology inclusion does not by itself make a statement canonical or empirically established.

## Open scope questions

The following remain intentionally unresolved during the initial drafting stage:

- Which concepts belong in the ontology core versus specialized modules?
- Which AI Foundations terms should be modeled as classes, properties, processes, states, individuals, or annotations?
- Which external ontologies, if any, should later be reused or aligned with?
- What level of formal reasoning should v1.0 support?

These questions should be resolved through ontology construction rather than assumed in advance.
